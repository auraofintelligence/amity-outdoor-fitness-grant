# Consultation plan — simple and grant-friendly

## Purpose

Show that the project is wanted, useful, inclusive and respectful before asking for major funding.

## Who to speak with

- Redland City Council officers
- Amity residents
- local community organisations
- Amity Point Community Club and nearby reserve users
- young people and skaters / scooter riders
- older residents
- women and girls
- people with disability or mobility needs
- local health, fitness, yoga, Tai chi or walking group leaders
- campers and visitors, if appropriate
- Quandamooka representatives through appropriate channels

## Plain questions

1. Would you use a free outdoor fitness and stretching circuit in Amity?
2. What would make it feel safe and welcoming?
3. What equipment would you actually use?
4. What should be avoided?
5. Is shade, seating, water or access more important than extra equipment?
6. Would you use QR workout signs?
7. Would you attend a free launch class or community session?
8. What would help older people, young people, women and beginners use it?
9. What concerns do you have about the site?
10. Who else should be asked?

## Consultation evidence to collect

- number of people spoken to
- number of survey responses
- support letters
- photos of consultation sessions
- quotes from residents, with permission
- list of changes made because of feedback
- notes from Council and stakeholder meetings

## Good grant sentence

Community feedback will be used to refine the layout, choose inclusive equipment, identify access needs and confirm that the project is useful, safe and maintainable for Amity.
