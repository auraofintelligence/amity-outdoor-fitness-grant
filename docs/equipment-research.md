# Equipment research — coastal outdoor fitness and mind-body options

## Design principle

Build a public space that feels useful every day, not a fancy gym that becomes hard to maintain.

For Amity, the safest grant position is:

- simple fixed equipment first
- low moving parts
- strong corrosion resistance
- clear beginner/intermediate/advanced instructions
- visible location and safe sightlines
- accessible paths and rest points
- shade and seating
- a calm mind-body zone for stretching and breathing

## Best equipment categories

### 1. Core bodyweight strength

Recommended:

- pull-up and chin-up bars
- parallel bars and dip rails
- push-up rails
- incline sit-up bench
- step-up and plyometric blocks
- Swedish ladder / rib wall
- monkey bar or traverse bar only if the fall-zone and safety case are strong

Why it works:

- cheap to use
- strong health logic
- simple to maintain
- suitable for different fitness levels
- easy to explain to grant assessors

### 2. Gentle mobility and balance

Recommended:

- stretch posts
- shoulder wheel
- balance rails
- low balance pods
- hand cycle or low-impact cycle, only if maintenance is funded
- assisted squat-to-stand station

Why it works:

- supports older adults and beginners
- helps with mobility, balance and confidence
- makes the project more inclusive than a youth-only gym

### 3. Youth-friendly challenge

Recommended:

- low challenge trail
- agility ground markings
- step sequence
- low rails and balance features
- link to the existing skate / scooter / bike activity in the reserve area

Avoid:

- risky homemade obstacle course elements
- high falls without proper surfacing
- complex moving parts that will fail in salt and sand

### 4. Mind-body and mental wellbeing

Recommended:

- yoga / Tai chi hardstand or deck
- meditation or quiet seating circle
- stretch and breathwork signs
- mindful walking path or small labyrinth
- nature-sensory prompts
- QR audio or visual routines

Why it works:

- broadens the project beyond gym equipment
- supports mental wellbeing and social connection
- gives older residents and gentle movers a clear reason to use the space
- creates a useful place even for people who do not do hard exercise

### 5. Access and amenities

Recommended:

- firm accessible loop path
- wheelchair turning spaces near selected stations
- shade
- seating
- water refill point if feasible
- bins
- bike racks
- simple signs
- maintenance contact
- clear sightlines from the road and existing paths

These features are not “extras”. They often decide whether people actually use the equipment.

## Coastal durability specification

Ask suppliers to confirm, in writing:

- equipment is suitable for coastal salt-air conditions
- hot-dip galvanised steel after fabrication or marine-grade stainless/aluminium construction
- 316 stainless steel tamper-resistant fasteners where suitable
- high-UV, marine-grade powder coating
- UV-stabilised plastic or composite parts
- sealed bearings if any moving parts are used
- replacement parts availability
- corrosion warranty
- inspection and maintenance schedule
- compliance with relevant public outdoor fitness equipment standards

## Suggested “happy medium” equipment list

1. multi-height pull-up bar
2. parallel bars / dip rails
3. push-up rails
4. incline sit-up bench
5. step-up / plyometric blocks
6. balance rail
7. stretch posts
8. shoulder mobility station
9. low-impact seated cycle or hand cycle, if maintenance is funded
10. yoga / Tai chi hardstand
11. QR workout sign cluster
12. accessible seating and shade

## Signage ideas

Each sign should be plain and practical:

- “Start easy. Stop if you feel pain. Use at your own ability.”
- “Beginner: 5 minutes. Intermediate: 15 minutes. Advanced: 30 minutes.”
- “Strength: two days a week is a good target.”
- “Balance and mobility: small movements count.”
- “Scan for bodyweight, stretch and mobility routines.”
- “Report damage to Council.”

## Mind-body sign prompts

Simple prompts:

- “Breathe in for four, out for six.”
- “Notice the breeze, trees and water.”
- “Move slowly. Keep your feet steady.”
- “Stretch gently. No force needed.”
- “One minute of calm is still a win.”

## What not to overpromise

Do not claim the equipment will solve health problems by itself. A stronger claim is:

> This project will remove practical barriers by providing free, visible, low-maintenance infrastructure that supports physical activity, mobility, wellbeing and social connection.
