# Grant brief — Amity Point Outdoor Fitness & Wellbeing Circuit

## Plain project name

**Amity Point Outdoor Fitness & Wellbeing Circuit**

## Proposed location

Amity Point Recreation Reserve, Claytons Road, Amity, Minjerribah / North Stradbroke Island.

The exact footprint must be confirmed with Redland City Council before any grant is lodged. The screenshot area appears to sit near Claytons Road and existing recreation facilities. Council land tenure, underground services, trees, native title, cultural heritage, drainage, visibility, access and maintenance responsibilities all need checking.

## Simple grant pitch

This project will create a free, salt-resistant outdoor fitness and wellbeing circuit at Amity Point so residents and visitors can strengthen, stretch, move safely, meet others and enjoy healthy activity close to home.

## What we want to build

A small but useful public exercise and wellbeing space with:

- fixed bodyweight fitness stations such as pull-up bars, dip rails, step-up blocks, push-up rails and core benches
- mobility and gentle movement options such as stretch posts, balance rails and shoulder mobility equipment
- a calm mind-body area for stretching, yoga, Tai chi, breathing and recovery
- accessible paths, turning spaces and beginner/intermediate/advanced exercise options
- shade, seating, drinking water planning, bins, bike racks and clear signage
- QR-linked workout routines and simple safety information
- coastal-grade materials designed for salt, UV, sand and public use
- optional local art or place-based features, only after proper consultation and permission

## Why it matters

Amity is a coastal village with a strong outdoor lifestyle, but public fitness infrastructure can make healthy activity easier for people who do not have a gym membership, transport, equipment or a regular sport club. A visible public circuit also supports social connection, confidence, mobility and low-cost wellbeing.

The project is easy to explain in grant language:

- **free access:** no gym fee or booking required
- **health benefit:** strength, mobility and daily movement
- **social benefit:** a friendly place to meet, train or stretch
- **inclusive design:** options for young people, older adults, beginners, visitors and mobility users
- **local resilience:** durable infrastructure built for coastal conditions
- **measurable outcomes:** usage counts, launch sessions, QR scans, surveys and maintenance checks

## Main beneficiaries

- Amity residents and families
- older adults wanting balance, mobility and gentle strength
- young people wanting safe challenge and bodyweight training
- women and girls wanting visible, free and social exercise options
- skaters, walkers, campers and visitors already using the reserve area
- people without gym access
- community groups that may run low-cost sessions

## Recommended first serious build: “Happy medium”

Estimated project range: **$120,000–$180,000**.

This is big enough to create a real facility, but still plain enough for a grant assessor:

- 8–12 fixed stations
- accessible loop path and surfacing
- shade and seating
- simple signs and QR workouts
- mind-body stretch area
- design, freight, installation and maintenance allowance
- launch and evaluation programme

## What success looks like

Within the first year after installation:

- residents and visitors can use the space for free at any time
- local groups can run simple stretch, walking, mobility, Tai chi or bodyweight sessions
- QR signs provide safe beginner, intermediate and advanced routines
- usage can be counted through observation, surveys and QR scans
- the equipment remains clean, safe and maintained
- community feedback supports future improvements

## Simple outcome measures

| Outcome | Measure |
|---|---|
| More people moving | estimated visits per week, launch attendance, QR routine opens |
| Better access to strength and mobility | number of stations, beginner/intermediate/advanced exercise options |
| Social connection | group sessions, survey feedback, volunteer involvement |
| Inclusive local infrastructure | accessible approach paths, seating, shade, options for older adults and beginners |
| Long-term value | maintenance checks, low repair rate, supplier warranty, community use stories |

## Risks and how to reduce them

| Risk | Reduction |
|---|---|
| Salt corrosion | specify marine-grade or hot-dip galvanised materials, 316 fasteners, high-UV coatings and supplier corrosion warranty |
| Poor use | involve locals early, make signs simple, run launch sessions, include all-ability options |
| Safety issues | use certified public outdoor fitness equipment, compliant surfacing and clear maintenance checks |
| Approval delays | speak with Council before grant submission and obtain written landowner consent |
| Cultural or place mistakes | engage early and do not use cultural stories, names, art or symbols without permission |
| Maintenance burden | choose static equipment, budget for inspections and keep moving parts to a minimum |

## Plain closing line for a grant application

The Amity Point Outdoor Fitness & Wellbeing Circuit is a practical, visible and low-cost way to support healthier daily movement, social connection and accessible outdoor recreation in a coastal island community.
