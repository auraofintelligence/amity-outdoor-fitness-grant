Equipment

Start by picturing the gear people would enjoy using.

The park should feel welcoming, not like a row of hard gym bars. Some pieces can be simple and sturdy for everyday strength and stretching. Others can move, wobble or spin, so older locals, kids, visitors and regular walkers all see something made for them.

Two equipment lanes

Design the mix before choosing the supplier.

Static backbone

Fixed, simple, durable

  • pull-up bars, parallel bars, stretch stations, dip benches and low balance rails
  • best for low maintenance, clear fall zones and long asset life
  • good for QR routines that scale from beginner to advanced
Motion and sprung

More engaging, more checks

  • air walkers, bikes, cross trainers, rotating wheels, surfboard balance and rider-style equipment
  • better for older users, rehab-style movement, warm-ups, kids and visitors
  • needs stronger warranty, inspection, parts and corrosion questions
